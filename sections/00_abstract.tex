\begin{abstract}
This case study presents a rigorous empirical and theoretical comparison between a two-qubit variational quantum classifier (QNN/VQC) with data re-uploading and classical multilayer perceptrons (MLPs). We evaluate models across three controlled benchmarks: an overlapping, non-trivial botanical classification task (\emph{Iris versicolor} vs. \emph{Iris virginica}), the nonlinear Two Moons distribution, and a rotationally symmetric Concentric Circles task. The experiment employs ten paired stratified train/test splits, a compact 9-parameter classical network as a parameter-matched control, and a wider 193-parameter MLP as a practical baseline. We isolate architectural effects via circuit depth scaling (depths 1--3), an entanglement ablation removing CNOT gates, and multinomial finite-shot inference sampling (256 and 1,024 shots). Executed with PennyLane \texttt{default.qubit} (Torch interface, simulator backpropagation) and verified against an exact PyTorch statevector reference, the study demonstrates that classical MLPs consistently outperform or match QNNs on classical tabular datasets. On the realistic Iris benchmark, classical MLPs achieve $0.956\pm0.030$ (wide) and $0.960\pm0.027$ (compact) compared to $0.912\pm0.037$ for the depth-2 QNN ($p=0.0032$). On Two Moons, the primary depth-2 QNN achieves $0.763\pm0.030$ accuracy compared to $0.838\pm0.021$ for the compact MLP and $0.938\pm0.024$ for the wider MLP ($p < 10^{-5}$). However, on Concentric Circles, the periodic rotation encoding imparts a strong inductive bias: the depth-1 QNN ($0.980\pm0.017$) and depth-3 QNN ($0.977\pm0.023$) dramatically outperform the capacity-matched compact MLP ($0.584\pm0.109$, $p=0.0033$). We formulate the theoretical principles governing why classical NNs dominate classical learning (gradient dynamics, non-unitary activations, unconstrained parameter scaling) while detailing the four precise problem regimes where quantum models hold proven or plausible advantage (quantum-native data as cataloged in QDataSet, discrete-logarithm kernels, high-order parity, and classically hard quantum kernels). Turnkey reproducibility is provided via an automated cross-platform pipeline.
\end{abstract}
\vspace{0.04in}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} quantum machine learning, variational quantum circuit, QNN, inductive bias, classical neural network, QDataSet, PennyLane, PyTorch, benchmarking.
