\begin{abstract}
This case study presents a rigorous empirical and theoretical comparison between variational quantum classifiers (QNN/VQC) with data re-uploading and classical multilayer perceptrons (MLPs). We evaluate models across five domain-purposeful benchmark datasets organized into three distinct inductive-bias categories: (1) \emph{Quantum State \& Phase Classification} via the PennyLane Transverse-Field Ising Model (TFIM) and Qiskit Phase of Matter (Heisenberg spin chain); (2) \emph{High-Dimensional / Non-Linear Quantum Kernels} via the Havl\'i\v{c}ek quantum feature map benchmark (\texttt{ad\_hoc\_data}); and (3) \emph{Classical Small-Data \& Low-Sample Benchmarks} via Wisconsin Breast Cancer Diagnostic and downsampled MNIST handwritten digits (0 vs.\ 1). Systematic Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) precedes training to characterize column schemas, data types, target designations, class symmetry, geometric separation, inter-feature correlations, and principal component variance spectra. The experiment employs paired stratified train/test splits, capacity-matched compact classical networks as parameter-controlled baselines (9 parameters for 2D inputs, 13 parameters for 4D inputs), and wider MLPs (193 and 225 parameters) as expressive references. We isolate architectural effects via circuit depth scaling (depths 1--3), an entanglement ablation removing CNOT gates, and multinomial finite-shot inference sampling (256 and 1,024 shots). Executed with PennyLane \texttt{default.qubit} (Torch interface, simulator backpropagation) and verified against an exact PyTorch multi-qubit statevector reference, the study demonstrates that classical MLPs consistently outperform or match QNNs on generic classical tabular datasets. However, on geometric tasks aligned with quantum inductive biases---such as entangled quantum kernel manifolds and physical quantum phase transitions---variational quantum circuits match or outperform capacity-matched classical networks with exceptionally compact parameter counts ($\le 13$ parameters). We formulate the theoretical principles governing why classical NNs dominate classical learning while detailing the four precise problem regimes where quantum models hold proven or plausible advantage (quantum-native physical dynamics, discrete-logarithm kernels, high-order parity, and classically hard quantum feature maps). Turnkey reproducibility is provided via an automated cross-platform pipeline.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} quantum machine learning, variational quantum circuit, QNN, inductive bias, classical neural network, TFIM, Qiskit ML, Havl\'i\v{c}ek benchmark, PennyLane, PyTorch, exploratory data analysis.
