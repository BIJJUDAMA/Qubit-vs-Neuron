\section{Evaluation protocol}
The primary comparison evaluates the wide MLP versus the depth-2 entangled QNN across all four benchmarks. Secondary experiments compare QNN depths 1, 2, and 3, compare depth 2 with versus without CNOT, and contrast the QNN against parameter-matched compact MLPs ($d\to2\to1$: 9 parameters on 2D datasets, 13 parameters on 4D QDataSet). Metrics are accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, ROC AUC, binary log loss, and measured training time. Scores are reported as mean and sample standard deviation across ten paired runs.

For model comparisons, paired differences are calculated seed by seed:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_i = \text{Metric}_{\text{QNN}, i} - \text{Metric}_{\text{Classical}, i}.
\end{equation}
A paired $t$-test and Wilcoxon signed-rank test are calculated alongside a 95\% confidence interval for the mean paired difference. When every paired accuracy difference is exactly zero (as on the linearly separable Iris subset), the $t$-statistic is degenerate and Wilcoxon $p=1.0$.

\subsection{Runtime and implementation details}
The same full-batch training schedule is used for all models: binary cross-entropy with logits, Adam optimizer, 100 epochs, no mini-batch sampling, weight decay $10^{-4}$, and gradient clipping at norm 5. The MLP learning rate is 0.01 and the QNN learning rate is 0.025.

Elapsed training time is measured strictly around the model training call on a single CPU thread. The primary PennyLane backend executes a batched Torch-interface QNode on \texttt{default.qubit} with analytic expectation values (\texttt{shots=None}). The reference backend uses explicit PyTorch tensor statevectors. QNode dispatch overhead materially affects absolute timing, which is why measured PennyLane execution times are explicitly reported.

\subsection{Finite-shot evaluation}
To inspect measurement sampling variability, the trained depth-2 QNN is evaluated using exact expectations and simulated multinomial measurements with 256 and 1,024 shots across five independent resamplings per split. Model weights are not retrained under finite-shot noise; this isolates inference shot sensitivity from optimization noise.

\subsection{Decision boundaries and learning curves}
Figure~\ref{fig:boundaries} displays the learned boundaries for all four benchmark tasks on the seed-0 partition. On Iris, both models cleanly partition the linearly separable classes. On Two Moons, the classical MLP constructs a smooth, tightly hugging nonlinear boundary, whereas the depth-2 QNN produces a broader elliptic boundary that misclassifies the inner tips of the crescents. On Concentric Circles, however, the QNN's rotation encoding naturally generates circular level sets, whereas the compact MLP with only two ReLU hidden units cannot isolate the inner circle. On QDataSet 2-Qubit Dynamics, Figure~\ref{fig:boundaries} depicts the 2D projection slice across normalized single-qubit expectation features $\langle XI \rangle$ and $\langle IX \rangle$, demonstrating that both architectures accurately classify the transition boundary between unentangled local drift and bipartite interacting Hamiltonian dynamics.

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:loss}, both the classical MLP and the variational quantum classifier exhibit smooth, monotonic binary cross-entropy loss descent under Adam optimization with gradient clipping. The classical MLP converges substantially faster across all benchmarks, reaching asymptotic loss floors within 30 epochs. In contrast, the QNN exhibits gentler initial loss gradients due to trigonometric landscape curvature, but consistently converges to stable minima without catastrophic saddle-point stagnation across the 100-epoch budget.

\subsection{Finite-shot sensitivity}
To assess the vulnerability of trained variational classifiers to quantum measurement shot noise on physical hardware, we evaluated the depth-2 QNN under exact statevector expectations alongside simulated finite sampling budgets of 256 and 1,024 measurement shots. As reported in Table~\ref{tab:shots} and illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:shots}, performance remains remarkably resilient to statistical sampling fluctuation: accuracy drops by less than $0.5\%$ at 1,024 shots and remains within $1\%$ of analytic expectations even at 256 shots across all four tasks. This indicates that while finite measurement sampling introduces stochastic variance during inference, the learned decision boundaries possess sufficient margin to tolerate moderate Poisson shot fluctuations.

The paired tests confirm four critical empirical insights:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item On the overlapping Iris task (\emph{Iris versicolor} vs. \emph{Iris virginica}), both classical MLPs slightly outperform the depth-2 QNN ($0.956$ and $0.960$ vs. $0.912$, $p < 0.005$), demonstrating realistic non-saturated margins on real-world botanical features.
  \item On Two Moons, the classical MLPs statistically dominate the QNN regardless of parameter count ($p < 10^{-5}$).
  \item On Concentric Circles, when controlling for parameter capacity, the depth-2 QNN significantly outperforms the compact MLP ($+0.1864$ mean gain, $p = 0.00334$), and depths 1 and 3 reach near-perfect accuracy ($0.980$ and $0.977$).
  \item On QDataSet 2-qubit dynamics, the depth-2 QNN reliably captures physical non-local correlations with 13 parameters, scaling gracefully with circuit depth.
\end{enumerate}
